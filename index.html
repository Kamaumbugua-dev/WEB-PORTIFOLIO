About Me

Steven Kamau Mbugua — ML & Deep Learning Engineer and Founder of Axon Lattice Labs — operating at the intersection of machine intelligence and production systems engineering.

My work covers the complete ML lifecycle — from data architecture and feature engineering through neural network design, hyperparameter optimisation, model compression, and production deployment. Every system is built to last, not just to demonstrate.

I operate with equal fluency across research and engineering. The models I develop are designed for production from the first line of code — versioned, monitored, and instrumented to remain performant under real-world conditions and real data drift.

Every engagement is anchored to a business outcome. Model accuracy is a means to an end — that end being demonstrable improvements in conversion, operational efficiency, cost reduction, or user experience at scale.

Across classification, regression, natural language processing, computer vision, time-series forecasting, and recommendation systems, my work has served production workloads across multiple industries — each time with rigour, ownership, and measurable results.

FAQ

Common
Questions

Direct answers to the questions that arise most often in conversations with hiring managers, technical leads, and engineering directors.

What types of ML problems do you specialize in?+

My sweet spot is complex, high-impact ML problems that require both deep technical knowledge and strong engineering discipline. I've worked extensively on supervised learning (classification and regression at scale), deep neural networks for computer vision and NLP, recommendation systems, time-series forecasting for financial and operational data, anomaly detection, and large language model applications. I'm especially strong in situations where the model needs to go beyond accuracy — where fairness, explainability, latency, or cost constraints are also critical requirements.

How do you ensure models perform reliably in production?+

Production reliability is something I design for from day one. My approach includes comprehensive data validation at every pipeline stage, statistical drift detection for both feature and concept drift, shadow deployments and canary releases before full rollouts, clear rollback procedures with versioned artifacts in MLflow or W&B, automated retraining triggers when performance degrades, and alerting tied to business metrics — not just technical ones. I've maintained models with 99.9% uptime SLAs in high-throughput environments.

Can you handle both research and engineering sides of ML?+

Yes — and this dual capability is one of my strongest differentiators. On the research side, I read papers regularly, implement novel architectures from scratch, and have reproduced results from top venues like NeurIPS and ICLR. On the engineering side, I write production code with proper testing, CI/CD, documentation, and operational runbooks. I know when to use a simple logistic regression versus when a transformer is justified. The best ML engineer picks the right tool for the job — not the most impressive-sounding one.

What measurable impact have you delivered?+

Concrete outcomes include: 38% improvement in classification accuracy for a customer churn model that directly reduced monthly customer loss; 45% reduction in inference latency through model quantization and batching optimization; 60% reduction in false positive rate for a fraud detection system; a demand forecasting pipeline that reduced inventory waste by 22%; and a computer vision quality control system that replaced manual inspection at 10x the throughput. I always tie my work to metrics that appear in business reports.

Are you open to remote, hybrid, or relocation opportunities?+

Absolutely. I have experience working in fully distributed teams across multiple time zones and have developed strong async communication practices. I'm comfortable with tools like Notion, Linear, Slack, and GitHub for remote-first workflows. For the right opportunity, I'm also open to hybrid arrangements or relocation, both locally and internationally. The best opportunities don't come with geographic constraints.

How do you stay current with the rapidly evolving AI/ML landscape?+

I maintain a daily reading habit of arXiv papers in ML, NLP, and systems areas. I follow practitioners at top labs on social media and in open-source communities. I contribute to or closely follow major open-source projects (Hugging Face, PyTorch, scikit-learn). I complete hands-on implementations of new techniques within weeks of publication. I also actively participate in Kaggle competitions, which gives me practical feedback on new approaches in competitive settings.

What is your experience with data engineering and pipelines?+

I treat data infrastructure as a first-class concern. I've built end-to-end data pipelines using Apache Airflow, Prefect, and dbt for transformation. I'm experienced with both batch and streaming architectures (Kafka, Spark Structured Streaming). I design data contracts and schemas upfront, implement data quality checks at multiple stages, and build data lineage documentation. I'm comfortable working with data at varying scales — from gigabytes on single machines to petabyte-scale distributed systems on Spark/BigQuery.

How do you approach working with cross-functional teams?+

I start every project by aligning deeply with product and business stakeholders on what "success" actually means — before writing a single line of model code. I maintain shared experiment tracking dashboards so anyone can see progress. I write technical specs that non-ML engineers can review and critique. I conduct demo sessions focused on business outcomes, not model internals. I've successfully partnered with product managers, data analysts, backend engineers, UX designers, legal/compliance, and C-suite stakeholders.
